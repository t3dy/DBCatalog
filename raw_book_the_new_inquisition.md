# The New Inquisition

**Card**: A polemical book about certainty, dogmatism, and the social costs of intellectual rigidity.

## Summary

Wilson's argument here is straightforward but not simplistic: institutions of certainty, whether scientific, religious, or political, can become inquisitorial when they stop treating models as provisional. The book critiques the desire for final answers and defends uncertainty as a more humane and intellectually honest stance.

## Major Themes

- anti-dogmatism
- intellectual freedom
- model-agnostic skepticism
- institutional rigidity
- the social consequences of certainty

## Form and Function

This is one of the most clearly argumentative RAW books. It gives the Illuminatus portal a nonfiction companion to the trilogy's own skepticism about systems that pretend to totalize reality.

## Relation to Illuminatus and Counterculture

The title itself resonates with the trilogy's distrust of hidden authority. The book helps explain why Wilson's satire so often targets institutions that present themselves as final arbiters of truth.
