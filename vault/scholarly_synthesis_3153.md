## The Core Concept
An analysis of Jensen Suther's review of Anna Kornbluh’s *Immediacy, or, The Style of Too Late Capitalism*. The synthesis focuses on the tension between "immediatist" aesthetics (which prioritize raw affect, presence, and anti-mediation) and dialectical reason. Kornbluh argues that contemporary culture's obsession with immediacy is a symptom of late capitalism's drive to eliminate friction and mediation.

## Key Arguments
1. **Affect vs. Reason**: Immediatism elevates personal, unmediated feeling over structural, mediated thinking. 
2. **The Style of Late Capitalism**: The seamlessness of modern UI, the gig economy, and autofiction all share the aesthetic of "immediacy," erasing the labor and institutions that produce them.
3. **The Defense of Mediation**: Suther and Kornbluh both advocate for a return to mediation—institutions, structural critique, and formal representation—as the only way to politically organize against capital.
