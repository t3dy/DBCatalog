# Wilhelm Reich in Hell

**Card**: A satirical and political book that uses the afterlife format to think through science, repression, and public misunderstanding.

## Summary

The book imagines a posthumous environment in which Wilhelm Reich's ideas, scandals, and cultural afterlives can be staged with comic seriousness. RAW uses the premise to explore how controversial thinkers get transformed into symbols, targets, and myths after death. The work is both playful and polemical.

## Major Themes

- scandal and afterlife
- political imagination
- repression and liberation
- the myth of the controversial thinker
- satire as intellectual commentary

## Form and Function

This book belongs in the RAW archive because it shows Wilson using fiction to think about historical reception. It is not just about Reich; it is about what happens when a controversial intellectual becomes a cultural object.

## Relation to Illuminatus and Counterculture

Its satirical edge fits naturally with Wilson's broader countercultural stance. The book helps readers understand how RAW extends the same anti-dogmatic method across fiction and essay.
