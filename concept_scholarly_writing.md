# Master Concept: Scholarly Writing & Voice

This document serves as the global style guide for all AI-assisted writing within our ecosystem. Its goal is to guarantee that machine-generated prose maintains the weight, specificity, and density expected of serious university-level scholarship.

> **Glossary for Undergraduates**:
> - **Epistemological**: Having to do with *epistemology*, which is the philosophy of how we know what we know. In this context, it means we care deeply about *how* a scholar arrived at their conclusion, not just what the conclusion is.
> - **Marxist/Psychoanalytic/Formalist**: These are "theoretical lenses." A Marxist lens looks at a text focusing on class struggle and money; Psychoanalytic focuses on subconscious desires; Formalist ignores the author entirely and just looks at the structure of the words.

## 1. The Epistemological Stance

- **Never Flatten Contradictions**: Avoid smoothing over opposing views into a bland "nuanced middle ground." If Scholar A views a text as literal history and Scholar B argues it is purely symbolic, you must explicitly name and preserve this fight.
- **Lower Heaven into Reach**: True academic rigor does not require impenetrable, confusing jargon. Write with high conceptual density (packing a lot of meaning into a few words), but maintain clear, direct momentum.
- **Methodology Must Be Visible**: Readers must understand *how* a claim was constructed. Always attribute analytical claims to their specific theoretical lens (e.g., "Through a Marxist lens, this represents...").

## 2. The Scholar Profile Standard

When an AI generates a summary of a researcher or author, it MUST adhere to this strict structure to avoid sounding like a generic Wikipedia article:

* **Central Claim (1 sentence)**: What is the single, most distinctive argument this scholar is famous for?
* **Methodological Frame (1 sentence)**: Define their theoretical approach (e.g., Marxist, deconstructive, historicist).
* **Specific Moves (2-4 sentences)**: What new concepts did they introduce? What prevailing academic mistakes did they correct? Explicitly name their major books.
* **Scholarly Lineage & Disputes**: Map their intellectual family tree and their enemies. Explicitly state who they disagree with (e.g., *"Disputes: Sutin over the chronology of the Vancouver trip."*).
* **Relevance**: Tell the reader exactly when they should use this scholar (e.g., *"Consult for ideological-critique readings of the mid-1960s novels."*).
* **Quotable Evidence**: Provide 2-5 specific, exact text quotes to prove your analysis.

## 3. The Lint Checklist for Agents

Before an AI is allowed to save writing to the database, it must independently verify its output against this checklist:

- [ ] Is the tone critical and reportorial (acting like a neutral journalist reporting on an academic fight), rather than universally praising the subject?
- [ ] Are specific, actionable academic questions named, avoiding generic filler like "provides valuable insights"?
- [ ] Are all academic disputes explicitly blamed on named intellectual opponents?
- [ ] Is the analysis grounded by verbatim (exact word-for-word) textual evidence rather than paraphrased summaries?
