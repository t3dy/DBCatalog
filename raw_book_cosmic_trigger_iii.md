# Cosmic Trigger III: My Life After Death

**Card**: The late-volume continuation of Wilson's memoir sequence, shaped by reflection on mortality, memory, and what remains after the writer's public life has already become legend.

## Summary

This volume continues the autobiography-as-inquiry format while turning more explicitly toward aging, bodily limitation, and retrospective meaning-making. The extracted structure begins with a prologue and moves into a section called "The Masks of Reality," which fits the book's method: it reflects on how perception, persona, and narrative identity are all masks that can be revised. The title signals a playful but serious problem: what does a life become after the original narrative of selfhood has already been constructed by earlier books and by public reception?

## Major Themes

- mortality and retrospection
- the afterlife of the authorial self
- memory as reconstruction
- continued model-shifting
- the persistence of play under constraint
- reality masks
- public legacy

## Form and Function

This book matters to the Illuminatus portal because it shows how Wilson's voice remains active even when the life narrative turns reflective. The trilogy's unstable epistemology did not disappear in late life; it matured into a more self-aware commentary on authorship, body, and legacy.

## Relation to Illuminatus and Counterculture

The counterculture here is no longer youthful rebellion but a historical field the author is now remembering from inside a later stage of life. That gives the book archival value as well as literary value: it helps readers see how RAW understood his own trajectory after the major public myths were already in circulation.
