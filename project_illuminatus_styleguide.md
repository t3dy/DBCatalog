# Style Guide: Illuminatus! Trilogy Knowledge Portal

This guide defines the writing standard for the portal. The goal is an academic encyclopedia style: concise where possible, dense where necessary, careful about evidence, and explicit about interpretive status.

## 1. Global Writing Standards

- Write in a neutral scholarly register.
- Prefer clear claims over decorative prose.
- Distinguish fact, inference, and interpretation.
- Do not flatten disagreements.
- Do not imply occult claims are verified facts unless the source explicitly frames them that way.
- Use short paragraphs and strong internal headings.
- Every substantial page should answer four questions:
  - What is it?
  - Why does it matter?
  - How is it connected?
  - What is still uncertain?

## 2. Universal Entry Anatomy

Every good entry should follow this order unless a section type clearly requires a different structure:

1. Lead sentence or paragraph
2. Basic identification
3. Narrative or conceptual function
4. Key interpretive stakes
5. Cross-links and relations
6. Source notes or uncertainty markers

## 3. Word Count Targets

## 4. Section Templates

### A. Character Entries

Required subsections:
- Identity
- Narrative role
- Major scenes
- Alliances and antagonisms
- Esoteric associations
- Interpretive note

### B. Concept Entries

Required subsections:
- Definition
- Occurrences in the text
- Why it matters
- Relation to Crowleyan or Qabalistic material
- Related terms
- Caveats

### C. Plot-Focus Pages

Required subsections:
- Reading lens
- Textual range
- Main argument
- Key scenes
- Competing interpretations
- Related pages

### D. Routine Entries

Required subsections:
- Routine definition
- Repetition pattern
- Burroughsian frame
- Narrative or ideological function
- Disruption or failure mode
- Related routines and scenes

### E. RAW Biography and Works

Required subsections:
- Basic biography
- Publication history
- Counterculture and esoteric context
- Major works
- Reception and afterlife
- Related media and archives

### F. Map Pages

Required subsections:
- Atlas purpose
- Spatial scope
- Layer logic
- Why this map exists
- How to read it
- Related maps
- Key limitations

## 5. RAW Book Writing Standard

Every RAW book page should contain:
- bibliographic identification
- one-paragraph synopsis
- major themes
- genre or formal mode
- relation to RAW's broader oeuvre
- relation to Illuminatus! or to the wider esoteric/counterculture context when relevant
- note on uncertainty if the book is not fully in hand

Avoid summary drift. The page should tell the reader what the book actually does, not what a generic RAW title might do.

## 6. Media and Archive Standard

For internet writings, zines, video, and audio:
- identify the medium first
- give the best available date or date range
- summarize the content at the level of topic and argument
- mark incomplete material with a placeholder
- do not fabricate bibliographic precision

Use one of these placeholder forms when necessary:
- `[Need to add more bibliographic detail]`
- `[Need to add more interview detail]`
- `[Need to add more transcript detail]`
- `[Need to add more zine metadata]`
- `[Need to add more source confirmation]`

## 7. Quality Bar

A strong entry:
- has a specific thesis
- avoids generic praise
- names tensions and disagreements
- links to at least three related entities when possible
- uses source-aware language
- can be understood quickly as a card and deeply as a page

An entry is not good if it:
- repeats the same point in different words
- uses vague enthusiasm instead of analysis
- collapses source uncertainty into certainty
- forces a long essay where a short card would do
